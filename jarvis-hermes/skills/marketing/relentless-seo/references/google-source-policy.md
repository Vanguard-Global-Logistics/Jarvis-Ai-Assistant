# Google source policy

Use current primary sources before asserting how Google Search or Business Profile behaves.

## Preferred sources

- SEO foundations: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide>
- Helpful content: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content>
- AI search: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide>
- Spam policies: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies>
- Structured data: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data>
- LocalBusiness data: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business>
- Local ranking: <https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091>
- Search Console: <https://developers.google.com/search/docs/monitor-debug/search-console-start>

Verify the live source and access date because platform rules change. Cite the exact page supporting
each material claim.

## Interpretation rules

- Google does not guarantee crawling, indexing, rich results, local-pack placement, or ranking.
- AI Overviews and AI Mode still rely on core Search systems; do not sell unsupported AEO/GEO hacks.
- Do not claim `llms.txt`, content chunking, AI-only rewriting, or inauthentic mentions improve Google
  visibility without new primary evidence.
- Generative AI may help research and structure original work, but scaled low-value pages may violate
  spam policy.
- Structured data must be current, visible, relevant, and truthful; validation creates eligibility,
  not guaranteed display.
- Local improvement begins with complete and accurate business information. Distance, relevance,
  prominence, competition, and platform systems remain outside Jarvis's control.
